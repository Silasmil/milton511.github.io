    The Minimalist Home-Lab Playbook

    From zero to a working red-team lab in one weekend — one laptop, $0 in software, no hardware purchases. Seven chapters. Every step tested on the same Kali laptop you're probably using to read this.

    Why this exists

    Every "how do I start a home lab" tutorial on the internet makes one of three mistakes:

    • They assume hardware you don't have — "buy a Dell R710 for $300, set up Proxmox, add 64GB RAM."
    • They stop at "install Kali" — which is the first 5% of the lab. Nothing about what you actually do with it.
    • They skip the blue side — you learn to attack, never to detect. That's why so many home labs produce attackers who can't read a log.

    This playbook does the opposite. Everything runs on one laptop. Every chapter ends with a working exploit or a working detection. And chapter 7 is the one that turns the whole thing into a portfolio piece — which is what actually gets you hired.

    The seven chapters

    1. The stack

      VirtualBox, Kali, Metasploitable 2, Windows 10 eval — free, and installed in 45 minutes. Nothing else needed.

    2. The network

      Host-only + NAT adapter setup. Isolate your lab from your real network in a single diagram. Why the wrong adapter setting can leak your attacker VM onto the home Wi-Fi.

    3. The targets

      Metasploitable 2, DVWA, OWASP Juice Shop, one intentionally weak Windows VM. What each teaches, which to attack first, and why you don't need any of the other 30 "vulnerable VM" lists.

    4. The first exploit

      msfconsole → Metasploitable vsftpd → shell. Twenty minutes, end to end. The chapter where the lab stops being a download and starts being a lab.

    5. The pivot

      One compromised host, a chisel tunnel, a second host behind it. This is the chapter that separates hobbyists from practitioners — most tutorials never cover pivoting.

    6. The blue side

      Install a Wazuh agent (free) on the Windows target. Watch your own attack in the SIEM. This is the chapter that makes you useful in a SOC interview.

    7. The writeup

      Turn the whole thing into a portfolio piece. Exact template — the same one I used for all 9 Perfect Root writeups. Ends with a link to the public archive.
